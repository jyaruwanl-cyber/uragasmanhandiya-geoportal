ABOUT THE VILLAGE

A Digital Window Into Uragasmanhandiya

Uragasmanhandiya is a small coastal town and Grama Niladhari Division in the Karandeniya Divisional Secretariat of Galle District, Southern Province, Sri Lanka. Located roughly 80 km south of Colombo near the Indian Ocean, the town comprises three main administrative sub-divisions: Uragasmanhandiya North, South, and East.

According to the 2012 Census, the area had a combined population of 4,857, with a population density of about 700 persons per square kilometer. Demographically, it aligns with the broader Karandeniya region, featuring a predominantly Sinhalese and Buddhist community.

While Uragasmanhandiya remains largely agricultural and residential with limited industrial development, its location near neighboring hubs like Balapitiya, Bentota, and Ambalangoda provides economic advantages. Furthermore, its close proximity to popular southern eco-tourism attractions such as mangrove forests and sea turtle hatcheries in nearby Kosgoda and Ahungalla allows the town to contribute to and benefit from the region’s local tourism industry.

PLACES

Explore Important Locations

01

Kande Viharaya

Kande Viharaya is a major Buddhist temple situated in Aluthgama, Sri Lanka, founded in 1734 on top of a hill. It is world-renowned for housing one of the tallest sitting Buddha statues in the world, standing at about 48 meters (160 feet) high.

02

Kosgoda Turtle Hatchery

Kosgoda turtle hatchery, started in 1981, is one of the oldest turtle conservation projects in Sri Lanka. Kodgoda turtle hatchery is quite popular for having all five turtle species seen on the country's beaches. Functioning as a hatchery, Kosgoda projects envisage much more to preserve the turtle life on the shore. They run various activities and volunteer programs for locals and international tourists to go through and have a knowledge of processes of turtle hatching.

03

Andahelena Ella

Andahelena Ella is a charming 12-meter waterfall nestled within the lush Beraliya Mukalana Forest Reserve near Elpitiya in Sri Lanka's Southern Province. Surrounded by a dense, biodiversity-rich jungle canopy, it is particularly famous for its unique natural rock bathing pool located right at the top of the cascade. Accessible via a short, scenic trek through the forest from Pituwala village, it offers a peaceful and refreshing escape into nature.

CULTURE & IDENTITY

Tell the Story of the Village

The identity of Uragasmanhandiya is predominantly Sinhala Buddhist. Daily life, communal values, and local celebrations revolve around nearby ancient Buddhist temples (such as the Karandeniya Raja Maha Viharaya), village shrines, and seasonal Poya day observances.

The surrounding region played an important historical role in Sri Lanka’s cinnamon trade, particularly during the Dutch and British colonial periods. The local community has long-standing ties to traditional spice cultivation and agriculture, shaping both the regional economy and local customs.
